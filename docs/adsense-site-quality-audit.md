## Executive summary

iLoveTimers has more real utility than a superficial route-name review suggests. After Stage 3, the repository contains 125 canonical pages, 28 permanent redirect aliases, and one catch-all 404 response. Of the canonical pages, 102 are indexable and included in the XML sitemap; 23 working routes are intentionally `noindex,follow` and omitted from XML. The HTML sitemap and global footer contain all 125 canonical destinations.

The main site-quality problem is not an absence of text. It is the combination of:

1. **A very large number of closely related routes.** The largest intent clusters contain 12 generic countdowns, 11 interval/workout timers, 11 local digital-clock variants, nine date calculators, nine meeting/classroom/presentation tools, seven focus/study tools, seven event/date countdowns, and six world-time tools.
2. **Long, visibly templated supporting copy.** Forty canonical pages contain at least 1,800 supporting words, even though many are simple timers or clocks. Forty-seven retained canonical pages meet this audit's “substantial repeated content” threshold. The repeated structure is often `How it works` + `Keyboard shortcuts` + `Common scenarios` + `Frequently asked questions` + a disclaimer/“at a glance” block.
3. **Content that is long without adding route-specific decision value.** Repeated explanations of browser fullscreen, audio activation, shortcut focus, background throttling, and generic scenarios are useful once, but not when expanded into nearly identical long-form sections across dozens of routes.
4. **Several groups whose distinct route names exceeded their distinct functionality.** Stage 3 redirected the true functional duplicates, retained direct utility variants as `noindex,follow`, and preserved independently useful countdown, focus-cycle, meeting, workout, world-time, calculator, and seasonal workflows.
5. **Stage 1 found technical methodology and sourcing gaps.** Stage 2 added or revised visible methods on the 16 flagged calculation and technical routes, plus the debt repayment route. The remaining astronomy concern is broad reference-data validation, not an undisclosed formula.
6. **Stage 1 found copy-to-code conflicts and under-disclosed behavior.** Stage 2 corrected the Meeting Timer and Speedcubing copy, added strict time-zone and Unix date validation, and documented browser and device-sleep limits on the affected technical tools.
7. **Trust architecture exists and Stage 2 closed the key accuracy gaps.** Privacy now identifies the Astronomical Clock's exact localStorage payload and the Sunrise & Sunset Clock's external coordinate request. The process page states the real automated check scope. The 404 now has a title, noindex directive, and recovery navigation.
8. **The current ad layout is not the primary low-value signal.** There is no live AdSense script. Two fixed-size placeholders appear only on the homepage, below useful content, and no placeholders appear in active tools. The larger risk is page/intent quality, not ad density.

The next implementation direction is the later content rewrite and content proportionality work. Route architecture is now declared in `docs/route-architecture-manifest.json`; Stage 3 did not begin Stage 4.

## Stage 3 route-architecture register

Stage 3 assigns every one of the 153 addressable public paths exactly one status: 94 independent indexable routes (A), eight concise indexable presets (B), 23 non-indexable utilities (C), 21 direct permanent redirects (D, including the 17 existing aliases), and seven routes whose useful capability was consolidated before a permanent redirect (E). No route remains pending human decision (F).

- **Indexable presets (B):** `/seconds-timer`, `/tabata-timer`, `/tea-timer`, `/egg-timer`, `/pizza-timer`, `/new-year-countdown`, `/christmas-countdown`, and `/birthday-countdown`.
- **Non-indexable utilities (C):** the five fixed-minute preset routes; `/silent-timer`; `/focus-session-timer`; `/study-timer`; `/break-timer`; `/meeting-timer`; `/workout-timer`; `/rest-timer`; `/current-local-time`; `/minimalist-clock`; `/atomic-clock`; both debt routes; the three approximation-based astronomy routes; and the existing `/privacy`, `/terms`, and `/cookies` policies.
- **New direct redirects (D):** `/fullscreen-timer` to `/online-timer`, `/count-up-timer` to `/stopwatch`, `/cooking-timer` to `/kitchen-timer`, and `/world-clock-with-seconds` to `/world-clock`.
- **Consolidate then redirect (E):** `/boxing-timer` to `/round-timer`; three analog variants to `/analog-clock`; and three digital variants to `/digital-clock`. Round Timer gained boxing presets, Analog Clock gained the retained display controls, and Digital Clock gained the retained date/minimal settings before the source routes were redirected.
- **Debt decision:** both debt routes remain working and self-canonical with their Stage 2 disclosures, but are `noindex,follow` because their linear estimates and route names are not strong independent search landings.
- **Astronomy decision:** `/sunrise-sunset-clock` remains indexable because its API method and behavior are disclosed and tested. `/golden-hour-clock`, `/moon-phase-clock`, and `/astronomical-clock` remain working but are `noindex,follow` because broad reference-data validation of their approximations is incomplete.

Redirect aliases are absent from canonical route configuration, XML, the HTML sitemap groups, the footer, More-directory results, and rendered internal links. Non-indexable utilities remain available directly and in the complete footer/HTML directory, but are excluded from XML and prominent More/related-tool discovery. Every retained indexable tool has three to five architecture-filtered contextual links.

## Audit method and evidence

The audit used:

- `app/routes.ts` as the configured canonical route source.
- `app/config/redirects.js`, `public/_redirects`, and `netlify.toml` for static redirect behavior.
- `public/sitemap.xml`, `app/routes/sitemap.tsx`, `app/clients/config/siteDirectory.js`, the More directory, footer, and related-tool configuration for discoverability.
- The prerendered production HTML for every canonical page for titles, descriptions, canonicals, robots directives, H1s, visible controls/defaults, output text, supporting-word counts, and internal links.
- The actual route imports and component source for timing engines, calculations, storage, parameters, validation, audio, fullscreen, keyboard controls, visibility handling, and structured data.
- `app/clients/lib/calculatorMath.js`, `app/clients/lib/specialtyMath.js`, and their tests for calculation policies and edge cases.
- Rendered desktop and 390px mobile browser checks in light and dark themes.
- Reproducible extraction in `scripts/audits/adsense-site-quality-audit.mjs` plus rendered architecture validation in `scripts/audits/route-architecture.mjs`.

The worktree was clean when Stage 1 began. Stage 3 began with the complete, uncommitted Stage 2 change set already present; those pre-existing changes were preserved while the route-architecture implementation was added.

### Counting conventions

- **Canonical route:** a page declared in `app/routes.ts`.
- **Indexable route:** a canonical page without `noindex`; redirect aliases and the HTTP 404 response are not counted as indexable pages.
- **Supporting words:** rendered words after the page's visible H1. This excludes the global header/footer but can include route-specific FAQ, related-link, and methodology sections.
- **Contextual incoming link:** a static in-page link outside the global header, More directory, footer, route-aware global related-tools band, and HTML sitemap.
- **Substantial repetition:** a route with at least three legacy template component roles, at least three highly repeated headings, or at least four broadly repeated phrase patterns.
- **Claim issue:** a direct copy/code conflict, potentially misleading name or presentation, subjective unverifiable claim, or technical assertion that materially needs a primary source. Missing ordinary timer instructions are not counted as claim issues.

## Actual tool behavior

### Site-wide behavior that applies to most interactive tools

- There is no Notification API, service-worker notification, push-notification, Wake Lock, SharedWorker, or BroadcastChannel implementation anywhere in the repository.
- Closing a tab stops every in-memory timer. Device sleep can suspend display work, Web Audio delivery, and completion callbacks. A service worker is not a substitute for a durable alarm, and none is installed.
- Multiple tabs run independent live state. Pages that use localStorage can overwrite the same saved payload from different tabs, but no live-session coordination is implemented.
- Most countdowns and stopwatches derive elapsed or remaining time from `performance.now()`, `Date.now()`, a target timestamp, or a fresh `Date` read instead of merely subtracting one on each interval. This reduces accumulated interval drift. It does not guarantee that the display, beep, or completion callback occurs while a tab is throttled or a device is asleep.
- Most clocks read the current device clock on each scheduled update. They therefore reflect device/system clock error and changes. They are not external time references.
- Browser audio is generated with Web Audio or HTML audio. User activation, audio-context state, mute/volume, background policy, operating-system interruption, and device sleep can prevent or delay sound.
- Fullscreen uses the browser Fullscreen API and normally requires a user gesture. It changes presentation, not the timing source.
- Keyboard shortcuts commonly ignore typing targets. Several legacy pages also require focus on the tool card; this requirement is repeated heavily in the content.
- Active state is lost on refresh unless a route explicitly persists it. Storage behavior is listed below.

### Debt and astronomical methodology

- `/debt-clock`: `yearly change / 31,557,600` produces a constant per-second change using a 365.25-day year. There is no live source or compounding.
- `/debt-repayment-timer`: `progress = clamp((now - start)/(end - start), 0, 1)` and `estimated paid = (starting - target) × progress`. There is no repayment model.
- `/astronomical-clock`: local sunrise/sunset, solar-altitude thresholds, and mean-synodic moon approximation. Coordinates and time zone affect output. - `/moon-phase-clock`: fixed new-moon reference epoch plus mean synodic month.

### PWA/offline assessment

The current architecture can be cached because most tools are client-side React routes with deterministic local assets after the initial server response. Basic timers, stopwatches, clocks, converters, and calculators could operate offline once their page bundles are cached.

Current state:

- no web app manifest;
- no service worker or Cache Storage strategy;
- no install prompt handling;
- no offline fallback;
- no background sync/push path.

Offline would not make alarms reliable after the tab closes, prevent device sleep, guarantee audio, or keep service workers alive. Time-zone behavior would use the browser's bundled data, which can become stale. External analytics/fonts/images would need cache/failure policy. SSR route chunks create versioned cache invalidation and partial-update risks. A stale cached calculator or astronomy formula is a trust issue.

Recommendation: defer PWA work until route/content consolidation and a versioned cache/update test plan exist. If implemented, begin with a small explicit offline set and an update notification; do not market it as background alarms.
